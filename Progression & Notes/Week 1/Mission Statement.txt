This study presents a conceptual design of a 1U CubeSat intended to support monitoring of Arctic ice sheet albedo reduction and melt dynamics through hyperspectral imaging across the visible to short-wave infrared range (400–2500 nm). The satellite is designed to detect spectral signatures associated with light-absorbing impurities — including glacial algae, aeolian dust, and wildfire soot deposition — and variations in ice grain size that collectively drive accelerated surface melt beyond that attributable to temperature alone. Operating from a Sun-synchronous orbit at approximately 550 km altitude, the mission explores the practical limits of the 1U form factor as a low-cost platform for cryospheric remote sensing, contributing to the growing need for high-revisit, accessible monitoring of Arctic ice dynamics in the context of climate change.
