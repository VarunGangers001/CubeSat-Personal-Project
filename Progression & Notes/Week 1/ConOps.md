Following ejection from the launch vehicle's deployer into a Sun-synchronous orbit at approximately 550 km, the satellite undergoes an initial detumbling phase, using passive magnetic damping or active magnetorquers to stabilise its attitude. Once stable, the on-board computer transitions the satellite into an imaging mode during daylight passes over target regions, with the camera pointing toward the Earth's surface. Acquired images are stored in on-board flash memory until the satellite passes within range of a UHF ground station, at which point the stored data is downlinked. Throughout the orbit, body-mounted solar cells generate power, with a lithium-ion battery sustaining all systems during the approximately 33-minute eclipse phase. The on-board computer governs all mode transitions through a simple state machine, cycling between detumble, safe, imaging, downlink and charging states based on orbital position and system status
